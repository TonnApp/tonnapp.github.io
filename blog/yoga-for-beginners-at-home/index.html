Yoga for Beginners at Home: A Simple 30-Day Plan

Published: 2026-11-15 · 3 min read

You do not need a studio, special equipment or flexibility to start yoga. A quiet corner, a mat (or a firm rug) and 10–15 minutes are enough. This guide shows what to prepare, which poses to learn first and how to build a 30-day habit that you can actually keep.

Before you start

  • Space and time. Choose a spot where you can stretch your arms out to the sides. Practise on an empty stomach or two hours after a meal.
  • Clothes. Anything that lets you move freely. Yoga is practised barefoot.
  • Goal. Aim for three to four sessions a week at first. Short regular practice beats one long session a month.
  • Breathing. Breathe through the nose, smoothly and without holding your breath. If you cannot breathe calmly in a pose, ease off.

Six poses to learn first

1. Downward-Facing Dog. Stretches the back of the legs and shoulders. Keep the knees slightly bent if the hamstrings are tight and push the floor away with your hands.

2. Cat-Cow. A gentle spine warm-up on hands and knees: round the back as you exhale, lift the chest as you inhale.

3. Sphinx. A soft backbend that opens the chest and strengthens the lower back without strain.

4. Low Lunge. Opens the hips and strengthens the legs. Keep the front knee over the ankle and the shoulders relaxed; lower the back knee to the floor if you need more support.

5. Child’s Pose. A resting pose to return to any time you need a break. Sit back onto your heels, stretch the arms forward and let the forehead rest on the floor.

6. Butterfly. A seated pose for the hips. Sit tall and let the knees drop naturally; do not push them down.

The 30-day plan

  • Days 1–7: foundations. 10–15 minutes. Learn the six poses above, hold each for 3–5 breaths and finish with a short rest lying on your back.
  • Days 8–14: add lunges and forward folds. 15 minutes. Repeat the sequence on both sides and add a minute of calm breathing at the end.
  • Days 15–21: strength and balance. 15–20 minutes. Hold poses a little longer (5–8 breaths).
  • Days 22–30: flow. 20 minutes. Link the poses into a smooth sequence and finish with five minutes of relaxation.

Take one or two rest days a week. If you miss a day, simply continue from where you stopped; do not try to catch up.

Common beginner mistakes

  • Forcing a pose to look like a photo. Depth is not the goal; comfortable breathing is.
  • Holding the breath or breathing in short gasps.
  • Skipping the warm-up and cool-down.
  • Practising through sharp pain. A stretch should feel like gentle tension, never like pain.
  • Comparing yourself with others. Everybody’s body is different.
