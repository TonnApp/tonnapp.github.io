How Many Push-Ups and Squats Should a Beginner Do Per Day?

Published: 2026-11-15 · 1 min read

There is no universal number, because the right amount depends on your fitness and recovery. What matters is starting at a level you can repeat with good form and increasing it gradually.

Find your starting point

Do one set of each exercise to near-fatigue with good form. Take about half of that number as your daily total, split into several short sets.

  • If you can do 10 push-ups, start with 5 per set, a few times a day.
  • If you can do 20 squats, begin with 10 per set.

A beginner plan

  • Weeks 1–2: 20–30 total repetitions of each exercise per day.
  • Weeks 3–4: 40–60 total repetitions.
  • Later: increase by about 10% a week while form stays clean.

Making it easier or harder

  • Easier: knee push-ups, push-ups with hands on a bench, squats to a chair.
  • Harder: slower tempo, wide or close-grip push-ups, jump squats.

Rest and recovery

Daily practice is possible if the volume is low. If you feel sore or tired, take a rest day or do a lighter day. Joint pain is a signal to stop.
