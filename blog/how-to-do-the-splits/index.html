How to Do the Splits: A Safe Step-by-Step Plan

Published: 2026-11-15 · 2 min read

The splits look impressive, but they are not a gift for the lucky few. With a safe routine and patience most healthy people can move closer to them. Be realistic though: it takes weeks or months, and the timeline depends on your starting flexibility, age and consistency.

Two kinds of splits

The front splits (one leg forward, one back) need flexible hamstrings and hip flexors. The middle (straddle) splits (legs to the sides) need open inner thighs. Choose one and train it first; the stretches overlap a little.

Warm up first

Never stretch cold muscles. Spend 5–10 minutes on brisk walking in place, leg swings, gentle squats and lunges until you feel warm. A warm muscle stretches further and with less risk.

The best stretches

  • Low lunge. Opens the hip flexors of the back leg. Keep the hips square.
  • Pyramid pose. A forward fold with straight front leg for the hamstrings.
  • Half splits. Hips back over the back knee, front leg straight, folding gently forward.
  • Butterfly. Opens the inner thighs, useful for the middle splits.

How to practise

  • Train three to four times a week; give the muscles a rest day between hard sessions.
  • Hold each stretch for 30–60 seconds, breathing slowly. On each exhale you can sink slightly deeper.
  • Never bounce. Bouncing triggers a protective reflex and increases the risk of a strain.
  • Use support: blocks, cushions or a folded blanket under the hands or hips. Support lets you relax, and relaxed muscles lengthen.
  • Do both sides, even if one is more flexible.

Common mistakes

  • Forcing it. Pain means stop. Aim for strong but tolerable tension.
  • Twisting the hips. In the front splits keep the hips square and the back toes pointing down or neatly turned, not rolled out.
  • Neglecting the hip flexors. Many people stretch only the hamstrings and then wonder why progress stalls.
  • Skipping rest. Flexibility improves during recovery. Overstretching irritates the muscles and slows progress.

A weekly template

Day 1: lunge and pyramid. Day 2: rest or a walk. Day 3: half splits and butterfly. Day 4: rest. Day 5: all stretches, a little longer. Weekends: gentle mobility. Track the distance between your hips and the floor so you can see progress.
