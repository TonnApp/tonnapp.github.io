What Is 1RM and How to Estimate It Safely

Published: 2026-11-15 · 1 min read

The one-repetition maximum, or 1RM, is the heaviest weight you can lift once with good form. It is a useful reference for planning training weights, but testing it directly is hard on the body and risky without a spotter.

Estimate it instead

You can estimate 1RM from a lighter set done close to failure using the Epley formula:

1RM ≈ weight × (1 + repetitions ÷ 30)

Example: you lift 60 kg for 8 repetitions. 60 × (1 + 8 ÷ 30) ≈ 76 kg. Treat the result as a rough estimate, because the formula is less accurate above about 10 repetitions.

How to use it

  • Strength: train at roughly 80–90% of 1RM for low repetitions.
  • Muscle growth: about 65–80% for moderate repetitions (6–12).
  • Endurance: lighter loads with higher repetitions.

These ranges are general guidelines, and individual response varies.

Safety

  • Warm up thoroughly.
  • Use a spotter or safety bars for heavy squats and bench presses.
  • Do not test a true maximum when you are tired or alone.

Track it over time

The number matters less than its trend. If your estimated 1RM rises over weeks, your program works. The TonnPower app, in development, shows an estimated 1RM using the Epley formula next to your personal records.
